Each chapter shows where we've landed, why, and what the alternatives were. Most chapters end in a card marked Your call — tap an option, add a note if you want to, and move on. Every call saves the moment you make it — to a shared sheet, so Cara and Nathan see the same answers on any device — and chapter 14 turns them into a table you can copy into chat or Notion.

Recorded beside each call. Pick "together" when you're running the session as one.

Two rules of thumb for reading it. First, "locked" means the groundwork is done (names registered, domains held); "proposed" means it's our recommendation and yours to change. Chapter 02

The name

AVEN has passed the hard tests already — unique on the register, company and business names secured, domains held. What's left is the story we tell about it, and that has two strands — both worth keeping.

Inspired by the words Avenue and Avenir, AVEN represents the road ahead. We help clients move forward — towards better investments, stronger returns and a brighter future.Cara — brand direction note, August 2026

Cara's strand is the road ahead: avenue and avenir, direction and future. The brand-direction strand is the haven: shelter at the end of that road, with avens — a green wildflower — giving the palette its reason. Neither cancels the other. The avenue is the journey; the haven is what it's for — though a Perth agency trades as Haven, so haven stays a feeling we evoke, never a word we lead with. Together they read as one sentence: AVEN is the road ahead, and the calm at the end of it.

How the name is said and written

  • AY-ven. Rhymes with haven. Never "ah-VEN".
  • AVEN in capitals everywhere — mark, wordmark and running text. Title-case Aven only in long-form prose such as the About page or the booklet, where repeated capitals would shout.
  • The public brand is AVEN Property. The descriptor Property Management rides beneath it on formal documents, search listings and the Google Business Profile — it's what people type when they're looking for us.
  • Never "Aven Realty", never "Aven PM" in customer-facing material.

Why it works

  • Four letters, two syllables — easy to say, spell and search.
  • Carries feeling (haven) and motion (avenue) without explaining either.
  • Not a surname, not a franchise, not a pun — it will still fit when the rent roll is many times this size.
  • The wordmark sets wide and calm in a serif; the name does half the design work on its own.
Your call
Which telling of the name leads in the brand book and on the website's About page?

Chapter 03

The organising idea

One line the whole business answers to. It has to be true of the service before it's true of the marketing, and Cara's mission already says it.

To make property management feel calm, clear and considered — protecting owners' assets and creating a positive experience for landlords, tenants and tradespeople alike, through proactive, structured management and genuine care.Cara — mission, business plan
Second draft — the idea, said three ways
Calm, by design.

Calm is the promise. Structure is why it's true. Every owner who switches agencies is worn down — chasing, surprises, silence. AVEN is the other side of it, and the calm isn't luck: we benchmark every property first, manage to published standards, and back the standards with written guarantees. That's calm you can hold us to.

Proof one
Published, guaranteed standards

Response times in writing — six of them guaranteed, with a fee consequence when we miss them. We haven't seen a local agency put it on paper.

Proof two
Always within reach

Every enquiry answered within a business day. A leading reason owners leave a manager is being ignored — so it's the guarantee that matters most.

Proof three
The manager who stays

A small rent roll, run by the person who signed you. No handovers, no re-telling your story to whoever's left.

What "by design" rules out

  • Calm as a mood — soft colours and nothing behind them.
  • Hype: "premium", "passionate", "exceptional", "stress-free", "seamless".
  • Promising outcomes we can't control (rent levels, tenant behaviour).

What it demands

  • A system behind every claim — the SLA page, the inspection standard, the arrears timeline.
  • Specifics over superlatives, every time.
  • The same calm in a hard conversation as in a welcome email.

The idea, restated — pick the register

  • Calm, by design. The incumbent: systems make the calm. Quietest and most ownable of the three.
  • Managing today. Building tomorrow. Cara's forward register — care now, growth later. The road ahead in six words.
  • Where your property is going matters. The conversational telling — reads like Cara across a kitchen table.

How to read this draft

  • The organising idea and the hero tagline can be different lines — one steers decisions, one goes on the masthead.
  • Whichever leads, the fused story holds: the road ahead is the journey, calm is what it feels like on the way.
  • The hero shortlist in chapter 04 feeds this call; settle the shortlist first.

Chapter 05

The owner's story

Brands are written for a person. Ours is written for the owner on the day they decide they've had enough of their current manager.

My purpose in starting is to build a boutique property management agency where quality, compliance and customer care are never compromised. We have created an environment where processes are done right, clients are valued, and every interaction reflects professionalism and integrity, to ensure consistent standards and genuine care for both landlords and tenants.Cara — why AVEN exists

Mum-and-dad investors

  • One or two properties, often a former home they still feel attached to.
  • Their first question is about the tenant, not the rent: will they look after it?
  • We answer with screening they can see, inspection invitations, and regular touchpoints that build trust.

Investors from further afield

  • Buying into an affordable market from another city or state.
  • They want the best return, sensible maintenance and a portal they can check from anywhere.
  • We answer with sharp marketing, a yearly written rent review, reliable trades and real-time reporting — confidence without proximity.
The arc the brand tells
Worn down → benchmarked → managed to a standard → calm. Every piece of marketing is a chapter of that arc. The free Property Performance Review is the first chapter: a straight read on whether their property is being managed reactively or to a structure.
Who we say no to
Not all business is good business. We don't take on properties where the owner won't bring the home up to standard within a reasonable window — it only damages the owner, the tenant and our name. A curated rent roll is what keeps the guarantees honest.

Chapter 06

The promise

Second draft, rebuilt the way the session asked: every guarantee and standard now carries its own box, so Cara can settle the final wording point by point instead of approving the lot in one go. Her current wording stands as the draft; the boxes save to the shared sheet as you type. The general ideas held in the session — the detail is what gets worked here. Fee amounts live in the fee schedule, not in this book.

Most of what we publish is a service standard — a response time we hold ourselves to on every file, every day. A small number of promises go further: real guarantees, backed by real money. If we ever fall short, just let us know — the fee is waived or reduced, no argument and no drawn-out process.

Service guarantees — if we don't deliver, you don't pay
Statement accuracy. Spot an incorrect charge on your statement and we waive your admin fee for that month.

Vacancy. If your property sits vacant more than a week longer than a published external benchmark, the leasing fee is halved — provided our pricing recommendations, both the initial price and any adjustments, were followed.

Inspection. Every routine inspection is completed within its due month. Miss it (other than a tenant-requested reschedule) and the inspection fee is waived.

Tenant placement. If a tenant breaks their lease within the first six months, you're not left out of pocket for the replacement leasing fee: the departing tenant covers the share matching the months left on their lease, and we absorb the rest.

Communication. Every enquiry — phone, email or SMS — answered within one business day. Ever have to chase us, and the admin fee is waived for the month.

Leasing updates. A progress update at least weekly during a marketing campaign. Miss a week and the admin fee is waived for the month.

Any guarantee that's triggered is credited to your account within five business days, subject to normal banking delays.

Service standards — how we work
Leasing. Home-open feedback within a business day; applications presented within three; lease issued within a business day of approval.

Onboarding. Welcome call within a business day; yearly written rent review; update rhythm set to your preference, hands-on or hands-off.

Tenancy. Renewals sent within five business days; pet and modification requests forwarded within two; breach notices within two.

Maintenance. Genuine emergencies attended within 24 hours as the law requires, without waiting on your approval to begin (we still contact you as soon as practicable); everything else quoted within a business day, trades instructed within four business hours of your approval; the tenant updated within a business day, and you within a business day of the invoice. flagged for the biggest rework

Inspections. Routine reports within five business days, final reports within two, with recommendations.

Arrears. Day one, SMS and email reminder; day two, breach notice and a further reminder; days three to six, daily reminders and at least two calls; from day seven, a weekly update to you until resolved. Anything falling due on a weekend is actioned the next business day.

Financial. Urgent disbursements within a business day; statement the day funds are disbursed; invoices within two business days; shortfall warnings at least two business days before due; refunds within two business days of approval.

Compliance. Smoke alarm and RCD service records updated within 24 hours of the report; insurance renewal reminders at least 30 days before expiry.

The rest of the promise
The extra mile. Reticulation tested through the warmer months · flexi-hoses and under-sink cabinets checked for the failure you'd otherwise find on the floor · tenants coached through simple fixes before a trade is called · every plumber asked to check all taps and fittings while on site · seasonal tenant reminders on contents insurance, watering, sprinkler bans, lawns, pool safety and storms.

Switching is our job, not yours. Thinking of moving to us from another agent? The switch is free and we handle it entirely — we contact your current manager and manage the whole transition, start to finish. And if it isn't right in the first three months, walk away with no exit fee — we're so confident in the service that you will be too.

Onboarding, built from experience. Every new client completes Cara's onboarding questionnaire at sign-up — built from years of doing this — and it starts with the question most agencies never ask: why are you leaving your current agent? From there it verifies ownership against the Certificate of Title, sets communication and involvement preferences, records leasing preferences, appliances and access, runs a compliance and safety audit (smoke alarms, RCDs, pool barriers, minimum security), verifies insurance with renewal reminders, sets maintenance thresholds and preferred trades, and captures the owner's long-term goals. The answers pre-fill the management authority and tailor the service from day one — the Onboarding standard above is the promise; the questionnaire is how it's kept. The full document lives in the operations workspace, deliberately not in this public book.

Type rules

  • Serif for the wordmark, taglines, pull quotes and chapter headings. Sans for everything else.
  • Headings in sentence case. Wide-tracked capitals only for small labels — eyebrows, the PROPERTY line, sign-board footers.
  • Taglines set straight. No italics on any line the brand wants remembered.
  • Body copy at a comfortable size with generous leading — calm is mostly white space.

Never

  • Bold serif headlines — the serif works at regular weight; bold makes it shout.
  • Condensed or display faces borrowed from another brand.
  • All-caps sentences. Exclamation marks.
  • Mixing the serif into UI labels or forms.

Chapter 11

Voice

AVEN has two registers, and both are real. The calm voice talks to an owner about their property. The real-talk voice is Cara after seventeen years, telling it straight — funny, candid, occasionally spicy. One framework holds both: a core that never changes, dials that move per channel, and a short list of nevers.

Core — always, in both registers

Always

  • Plain English. Short sentences. First person plural — "we", not "the agency".
  • Specific over superlative: a number, a timeframe, a named check.
  • Say it straight, with respect. Hard news delivered calmly, never softened into vagueness.
  • Australian spelling. No exclamation marks. Taglines straight.
  • Kind, always — including about tenants, trades and other agents.

Never

  • Promise outcomes we can't control — rent levels, tenant behaviour, the market.
  • Hype: premium, passionate, exceptional, stress-free, seamless.
  • Mock a tenant or an owner. Name a competitor.
  • Urgency theatre, ALL CAPS, emoji in owner-facing email or standards.
  • Sarcasm in a complaint reply. Ever.
  • Em dashes as a habit. Commas and full stops do the work; one dash a message at most.

Operating principles that show in the voice

  • Say it straight — with respect. Clear, honest communication always. No fluff, no avoidance, no unnecessary sharpness.
  • Transparency builds trust. Open, clear and honest — even when it's uncomfortable.
  • Details aren't optional. Correct spelling, polished presentation, proactive updates — even when there's nothing new to report.
  • Always be kind. No matter what. Professionalism and kindness go hand in hand.
